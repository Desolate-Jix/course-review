I want to share a brief market analysis of our main digital payment platforms 
worldwide. First, I will show you four types of platforms, then describe their 
advantages and disadvantages, and summarize all users' common pain points.
The first kind of platform is C2C or P2P platforms, such as PayPal and Venmo. 
These services make it very easy for two people to transfer money directly, and they 
operate in many countries, which means they have a large global user base. On the 
other hand, they often charge high transaction fees, and sometimes accounts can be 
frozen without warning. Although the platform may review transactions, they are not 
fully visible on a public chain, so users must trust the company.
Secondly, we turn to B2C e-commerce payment systems like Stripe and Alipay. 
These platforms provide merchants with a complete suite of services, including 
invoicing, settlement, and reconciliation, and they integrate seamlessly into online 
stores. However, they charge a percentage of each sale, and merchants cannot trace
the full path of funds once they leave the platform. As a result, small and medium 
businesses sometimes have the high entry cost.
Next, let us examine cross-border payment services such as Wise and Remitly. 
These services typically offer lower fees than traditional banks, and they send money
very quickly, giving users a good overall experience. Nevertheless, they still depend 
heavily on local fiat-菲亚特currency networks and must comply with complex 
regulations in each country. Therefore, they remain only partially decentralized.
Finally, we have centralized clearing networks like Visa and UnionPay. These 
networks are accepted nearly everywhere in the world and are extremely reliable 
thanks to their mature infrastructure. At the same time, their settlement cycles can be
long—sometimes taking days—and the entire system is closed and controlled by a 
handful of clearing houses. Consequently, all transaction data is held by a few large 
organizations.
In conclusion, although each payment platform offers clear benefits, today's market 
still struggles with several key issues. First, users do not truly own or control their 
own funds and data. Second, payment routes are not transparent, making verification
difficult. Third, platforms can change their rules anytime, leaving users unprotected. 
Furthermore, there is no way for users to define custom incentive mechanisms or to 
quantify their own contributions. Finally, traditional loyalty points cannot be 
transferred between platforms, lack on-chain proof, and have poor interoperability.
