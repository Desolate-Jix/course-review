### 原始文字层

````text
Today’s digital wallets are becoming more powerful. They are not just for sending or receiving money anymore. In the 
future, wallets will also help with identity, personal data, and even connect with government systems. To improve 
safety, many wallets are starting to use AI to stop fraud before it happens. We can also use biometrics like fingerprints 
or facial recognition, and even behavior like how we type, to confirm who we are. New ideas like zero-knowledge 
proofs and multi-party computation are being used to keep private info safe, even without showing it. As quantum 
computers get stronger, we will also need new encryption that can protect us from those threats. Lastly, many wallets 
will work with CBDCs (government digital money) and become part of national ID systems. So in the future, your 
wallet may be your bank, your ID, and your security tool — all in one.
Even though wallets are improving, there are still big problems. The biggest risk is still the user — people lose 
passwords, fall for fake websites, or make mistakes. Also, different wallets and blockchains don’t always follow the 
same security rules, which makes it easier for hackers. Some systems say they are decentralized, but they still rely on 
a few important servers. If those are attacked, everything can go down. Another issue is slow government rules — 
many countries still don’t have clear laws or ways to punish attacks. And more secure wallets can also be harder to 
use, which makes people avoid them. To sum up, wallet security is not only about adding more technology. It also 
needs better user habits, teamwork between developers, and stronger laws. Only by working together can we build 
safer digital wallets for the future.
````
